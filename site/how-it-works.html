How the inheritance vault works

Will & Key is a non-custodial smart contract. It does not detect death. It follows a timer, an heir's transaction and a challenge period that the owner can veto.

By the Will & Key project team · Published 10 August 2026 · Security status

1. The owner creates a vault

The owner connects a wallet, chooses a supported asset and names one beneficiary wallet. The owner also sets:

  • Inactivity period: how long the owner may go without checking in.
  • Challenge window: the time between a beneficiary's claim and settlement.
  • Absolute horizon: a long-stop date that prevents automated check-ins from extending the plan forever after the intended limit.

Each vault covers one asset and one beneficiary. An owner can create multiple vaults for different assets or heirs. The contract does not receive or store a seed phrase.

2. The owner remains in control

While the vault is active, the owner can check in, add funds, withdraw funds, change the beneficiary and adjust permitted timing settings. A check-in moves the inactivity deadline forward, subject to the absolute horizon. One batch transaction can refresh several owned vaults.

A wallet signature is the authority. A stolen owner key can therefore control the vault; the contract is designed for absence and key loss, not for recovering from key compromise.

3. The deadline passes

Nothing moves automatically when the inactivity deadline passes. Funds remain in the contract. The named beneficiary must notice the condition and submit an on-chain claim from the nominated wallet address. A stranger cannot initiate the claim.

4. The beneficiary initiates a claim

A valid claim changes the vault from active to claim-pending and starts the challenge window. The effective settlement fee is locked when the claim begins and can only fall before settlement, never rise above the vault's creation-time ceiling.

5. The owner gets a veto window

Before the absolute horizon, an authenticated owner action can cancel a pending claim and restart the clock. At or after the horizon, soft actions cannot create an endless denial loop. The owner must either close the vault by withdrawing everything or extend the horizon far enough into the future to restore a meaningful cooldown.

This distinction is important: the challenge window protects against a mistaken or premature claim, while the horizon protects the heir against an unattended automation loop or repeated last-second veto with no real extension.

6. Anyone can finalize after the challenge

If the challenge period ends without a valid veto, anyone may call the finalization function. The heir does not depend on a Will & Key server or employee being available. Settlement records the heir's entitlement in a pull-payment credit lane. The heir then withdraws that credit to an address they choose.

Using credits rather than sending funds during the state transition prevents a hostile or incompatible recipient contract from blocking settlement through a failed callback.

Fees

The current global claim-settlement fee is 0.5%. The bytecode caps it at 1%. Each vault records the global rate at creation as its personal ceiling; an administrator cannot later raise that vault above the ceiling. Deposits, owner withdrawals and check-ins have no Will & Key service fee, although every on-chain transaction still pays the network's gas charge.

Failure modes to plan for

  • Owner key stolen: the attacker has the owner's authority.
  • Beneficiary key lost after the owner is gone: the entitlement may become permanently inaccessible.
  • Unsupported token behavior: rebasing and unusual transfer behavior can break normal accounting assumptions.
  • Missed notifications: reminders are not part of contract correctness; owners and heirs must not rely on email as the only awareness mechanism.
  • Contract defect: the deployment is immutable, so a serious defect requires a new contract and migration.
Verification before value: read the security and audit page, inspect the deployed source and try the complete flow with a small amount. The contract has not completed an independent audit.
