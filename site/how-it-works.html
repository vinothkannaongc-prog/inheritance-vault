How the inheritance vault works

Will & Key is a non-custodial smart contract. It does not detect death. It follows a timer, an heir's transaction and a challenge period that the owner can veto.

By the Will & Key project team · Published 10 August 2026 · Updated 26 September 2026 · Security status

The owner creates a vault

The owner connects a wallet, chooses an asset and names one beneficiary wallet. The owner also sets:

  • Inactivity period: how long the owner may go without checking in, from 7 days to 10 years. It can be changed later.
  • Challenge window: the minimum time between a beneficiary's claim and settlement, during which the owner can veto, from 7 to 365 days. It is fixed at creation. We recommend at least 14 days, because a veto only counts once it is included on chain (see what cancels a claim).
  • Absolute horizon: a long-stop date. Check-ins can never push the deadline past it, which stops automated check-ins from extending the plan forever. It is not a guaranteed payout date; see the horizon below.

Each vault covers one asset and one beneficiary. An owner can create multiple vaults for different assets or heirs. The contract does not receive or store a seed phrase.

Which tokens are supported

Supported: ETH on Base, and plain ERC-20 tokens that live at a single contract address, move exactly the amount requested, never change a holder's balance by themselves, and have no transfer limits. The live contract does not check this. It accepts any token address, so choosing a supported token is up to you. The guarantees on this site do not hold for:

  • Tokens reachable through more than one address (double-entry or proxy tokens, or a native coin that also has an ERC-20 address): the administrator's surplus sweep can take every vault's balance in that token through the second address.
  • Rebasing, interest-bearing or reflection tokens whose balance grows on its own: the growth counts as surplus the administrator can sweep, and the heir inherits only the amount deposited.
  • Tokens that can debit the vault by more than it pays out, such as fee-on-top or negative-rebasing tokens: all vaults in one token share one balance, so the shortfall lands on whoever withdraws last, who can receive nothing.
  • Tokens with per-transfer caps, maximum-wallet limits or transfer cooldowns: a payout is withdrawn in one transfer, so a payout above the limit cannot be withdrawn, possibly ever.
  • NFTs (ERC-721 and ERC-1155), and more than one asset in a vault.

The owner remains in control

While the vault is active, the owner can check in, add funds, withdraw funds, change the beneficiary, change the inactivity period and extend the horizon. A check-in moves the inactivity deadline forward, but never past the absolute horizon: in the last inactivity period before the horizon a check-in still succeeds, yet can only move the deadline as far as the horizon itself. From the horizon on, check-ins are refused. One batch transaction can refresh several owned vaults; it skips any vault that has a claim pending or has passed its horizon.

A wallet signature is the authority. A stolen owner key can therefore control the vault; the contract is designed for absence and key loss, not for recovering from key compromise.

The deadline passes

Nothing moves automatically when the inactivity deadline passes. Funds remain in the contract. The named beneficiary must notice the condition and submit an on-chain claim from the nominated wallet address. A stranger cannot initiate the claim.

Nobody is watching for you. Will & Key sends no alerts of any kind: nobody will notify you if your timer expires or a claim is filed, and your wallet will not show a claim against your vault. Nobody notifies your heir either. The challenge window only helps an owner who looks during it, so size the inactivity period and the challenge window together to the longest time you might plausibly not open the app. The inactivity period can be changed later, but a longer period never moves the deadline past the horizon; the challenge window can never be changed.

The beneficiary initiates a claim

A valid claim changes the vault from active to claim-pending and starts the challenge window. The beneficiary names a payout address when starting the claim. It cannot be changed afterwards, so it must be checked before signing. The claim also caps the fee rate; see fees.

What cancels a claim

Before the horizon, each of these owner actions cancels a pending claim and restarts the clock: Veto claim (abortClaim), changing the heir, changing the inactivity period, installing a check-in chain, extending the horizon, or withdrawing any amount.

These do not cancel a claim: a check-in (it is refused while a claim is pending), a batch check-in (it skips the vault), a paper-chain check-in, a top-up, and ordinary activity in your wallet such as a swap or a transfer. Only transactions sent to the vault contract count.

At or after the horizon, while a claim is pending, Veto claim, changing the heir, changing the inactivity period and installing a check-in chain are refused, and a partial withdrawal still goes through but no longer cancels the claim: the heir simply inherits less. Only two things end a claim: extending the horizon to a date at least one full inactivity period after the moment the transaction is mined, or withdrawing the entire balance, which closes the vault.

The challenge window protects against a mistaken or premature claim, while the horizon protects the heir against an unattended automation loop or a repeated last-second veto with no real extension.

A veto only counts once it is included in a block. On Base that depends on the network's sequencer: if it is down or ignoring a transaction, the transaction can still be forced in through Ethereum, which can take up to about 12 hours. On other chains it depends on that chain's validators. Do not leave a veto to the last day.

The horizon is a long-stop, not a payout date

The absolute horizon is the last date to which check-ins can push the deadline. If the owner is still checking in when it arrives, the heir can start a claim at the horizon and finalize no earlier than the horizon plus the challenge window. That date is the earliest inheritance if the heir claims exactly at the horizon, not a guarantee: the heir still has to start the claim and then finalize it, and a living owner can still stop a claim past the horizon by extending the horizon (repeatable, at least one inactivity period ahead each time) or by withdrawing everything. Never give horizon extensions to automation.

Anyone can finalize after the challenge

When the challenge period has run, anyone may call the finalization function. The heir does not depend on a Will & Key server or employee being available. Finalizing is not automatic, and the end of the window is not a veto deadline: until a finalize transaction is mined, the owner's key can still cancel the claim with the actions above. Heirs should finalize promptly. Settlement records the heir's entitlement in a pull-payment credit lane. The heir then withdraws that credit to an address they choose.

Using credits rather than sending funds during the state transition prevents a hostile or incompatible recipient contract from blocking settlement through a failed callback.

Fees

The current global claim-settlement fee is 0.5%. The bytecode caps it at 1%. Deposits, owner withdrawals and check-ins have no Will & Key service fee, although every on-chain transaction still pays the network's gas charge. The rate a claim pays is set in steps:

  • A ceiling at creation. Each vault records the global rate in force when its creation transaction is mined, and its fee can never exceed that ceiling. The administrator can change the global rate with no delay, so the ceiling is whatever rate is in force in that block, which may differ from the rate shown when you signed.
  • Cuts apply only while in force. A vault gets a lower global rate only if it is still in force when the vault's claim starts or settles. A cut that is later reversed does not stay with the vault; the vault can again be charged up to its ceiling.
  • Capped when the claim starts. Starting a claim locks the lower of the vault's ceiling and the global rate at that moment. At settlement the lower of that locked rate and the global rate then in force applies, so a rise after the claim starts can never take the fee above the locked rate. A cut helps the heir only if it is still in force when the claim is finalized: the administrator can reverse a cut at any moment, with no delay.
  • No fee recipient, no fee. If no fee recipient is set at the moment a claim settles, no fee is taken. The reverse also holds: a claim started while no recipient was set is charged its locked rate if a recipient is set before settlement. A fee recipient is currently set, to the administrator's hardware wallet.

What the administrator can and cannot do

Since 24 September 2026 the administrator of both contracts, and the fee recipient, is a single Ledger hardware-wallet key, 0x883C821103B5415C53B11E584D3592205B5CdCA3. It is one key, not a multisig. The handover transactions are listed on the security page.

Failure modes to plan for

  • Owner key stolen: the attacker has the owner's authority.
  • Beneficiary key lost after the owner is gone: the entitlement becomes permanently inaccessible. Only the named heir can claim, nothing happens at the horizon, and no administrator rescue exists.
  • Nobody notices: Will & Key sends no alerts, so a claim or an expired timer goes unnoticed unless the owner or the heir checks. Heirs are not told when a vault becomes claimable.
  • Token issuer action: all vaults holding a token share one balance at the vault contract. An issuer with pause, blocklist, upgrade or wipe powers, such as Circle for USDC, can act against that address and freeze every user of the token at once. Claims still settle on schedule, and payouts wait until the freeze lifts. A wipe is a permanent loss the contract cannot prevent.
  • Unsupported token behavior: see which tokens are supported.
  • Paper-seed check-in chain (advanced, no app support): an optional hash chain lets whoever holds a paper seed keep the vault alive after the owner loses their wallet. The app cannot install or use it; the construction is in the check-in chain specification. A chain check-in counts only if it is mined strictly before the deadline: once the heir has started a claim, the chain cannot stop it and its values cannot be used while the claim is pending, so submit at least a day early. If the owner's wallet key later cancels the claim, unused values work again, so a leaked seed stays dangerous until a new chain is installed. Chain values are bearer credentials. They cannot withdraw, change the heir or veto a claim, but whoever holds the seed or any unused value can postpone the heir's claim by up to the declared number of uses times the inactivity period, never past the horizon. Use a fresh seed for every vault and every chain, and never re-install an old one.
  • Contract defect: the deployment is immutable, so a serious defect requires a new contract and migration. The creation pause cannot stop deposits into existing vaults; in a migration, owners should withdraw in full, which closes the vault.
